Origami Crane — Illustrated Fold-Test Draft

A replacement traditional-crane sequence with original diagrams. Flat landmarks have been checked; pocket operations and physical folding remain under review.

Tutorial under review

This replacement is a fold-test draft, not a physically verified tutorial. Exact flat landmarks have been checked, but the square-base collapse, petal pockets, inside reverses and wing opening still need hands-on testing. The drawings omit hidden layers and use schematic opening views. Use the illustrated resources below for an established tutorial today.

Materials

  • One square sheet of paper
  • A flat folding surface

No scissors or glue needed. New to folding? Read the beginner’s guide.

How to read these diagrams

Read the words with each numbered drawing. Grey is the resting paper and blue selects a flap; these colours do not represent the two paper faces. Blue dashed lines are active creases, dotted grey lines are existing references, and arrows show movement. Drawings may enlarge checkpoints. Pocket-opening views and the final pose are schematics, not a certified 3D layer simulation. Turn-over means flip the whole model; it does not mean rotate it on the table.

Draft steps — not physically tested

  1. Choose the starting face

    Use a thin two-colour square, about 15 cm along each side. Set it coloured side up, with one corner pointing away from you and the opposite corner toward you. The diagrams use grey and blue to identify parts, not the actual paper colours.

    Open step 1 diagram for zooming

  2. Crease from top to bottom

    Bring the left corner onto the right corner. Match the edges, press the fold, and open the square fully. This makes the crease joining the top and bottom corners.

    Open step 2 diagram for zooming

  3. Crease from left to right

    Bring the bottom corner onto the top corner. Press and open fully. Both diagonal creases now cross at the centre. Keep the coloured face up until the next step.

    Open step 3 diagram for zooming

  4. Turn the sheet over

    Flip the whole sheet left to right, keeping a corner at the top. The white face is now up. This turnover matters: the next creases must bend the opposite way from the diagonal creases.

    Open step 4 diagram for zooming

  5. Crease between opposite edge midpoints

    With white up, bring the lower-right edge onto the upper-left edge, crease and reopen. Then bring the lower-left edge onto the upper-right edge, crease and reopen. The new creases join opposite edge midpoints. All four lines meet at the centre; the diagonal creases rise when the white side faces up.

    Open step 5 diagram for zooming

  6. Collapse the square base

    Keep white up and the bottom corner nearest you. Bring the left and right corners inward and down toward the bottom corner along the existing creases. Let the top corner settle onto them while the centre rises into a closed top point. Flatten without adding a new crease. You need a small diamond with its closed corner at the top and four loose original corners together at the bottom. If it forms a triangle with a straight open top, reopen and check the crease directions.

    Open step 6 diagram for zooming

  7. Precrease the front lower edges

    Keep the closed point up. On the FRONT flap only, bring the lower-right sloping edge to the vertical centre crease; repeat with the lower-left edge. Keep the rear flaps in place. The two folded side corners meet on the centreline above the middle of the square base.

    Open step 7 diagram for zooming

  8. Mark the petal hinge

    Fold the small top triangle down over the two folded side flaps, through the top stack. Its crease runs horizontally between the points where those side folds meet the upper edges. Press this cross-crease; it will be the hinge for both petal openings. The closed top point folds down with the stack; no unchanged top point should stick up behind it.

    Open step 8 diagram for zooming

  9. Reopen the three precreases

    Unfold the top triangle and both side folds made in steps 7 and 8. Return to the square-base outline, closed point still up. Keep the diagonal side creases and horizontal hinge visible. Do not open the square base itself.

    Open step 9 diagram for zooming

  10. Open one front flap

    Lift just the frontmost loose bottom corner. Hold the rear layers against the table. Raise the selected corner above the horizontal hinge. This opens a pocket; allow its two side walls to bend along the creases rather than pulling the whole stack upward.

    Open step 10 diagram for zooming

  11. Flatten the first petal

    Continue moving the lifted corner upward. Guide both side walls inward so the long edges meet on the centreline. The upper parts of the side precreases must reverse their direction. Flatten the elongated front diamond. Its two shoulder points stay at the ends of the horizontal hinge; the rear square-base flaps remain behind it. Stop if a side buckles or you have lifted more than one loose corner.

    Open step 11 diagram for zooming

  12. Turn over to the unfinished side

    Turn the entire model over left to right. Keep the long raised point at the top and the loose corners at the bottom. You should see the unfinished square-base face in front, with the first petal extending behind it.

    Open step 12 diagram for zooming

  13. Precrease the second face

    On the newly exposed front flap, fold each lower sloping edge to the centreline as in step 7. Leave the completed petal behind it alone.

    Open step 13 diagram for zooming

  14. Reopen the second face and find the hinge

    Unfold BOTH side folds just made on the second face. Find the horizontal hinge already creased through the top stack in step 8; it joins the upper ends of these side creases. Keep the completed long petal behind the square-base face. Do not fold that long petal down to make another hinge.

    Open step 14 diagram for zooming

  15. Open the second petal

    Lift only the frontmost loose bottom corner on this side. Keep the completed petal behind it down. Open the pocket across the horizontal hinge, guide the side walls inward, and flatten the lifted corner upward as in steps 10 and 11. Do not pull a lower corner of the completed petal up again.

    Open step 15 diagram for zooming

  16. Check the bird base

    You should have a long diamond. Above the shoulder line are two broad flaps, one on the front and one on the back: these will be wings. Below that line are two separate narrow points, left and right: these will be neck and tail. Gently separate the two lower tips just enough to identify them, then close them again. If the lower part is one unseparated flap, return to the petal steps.

    Open step 16 diagram for zooming

  17. Narrow the front edges

    Fold each lower outer edge of the front layer toward the centreline. The fold starts at the bottom tip and continues upward into the shoulder area. Keep the two lower points separate; do not fold the whole diamond in half. Leave a hairline gap at the centre if the paper becomes crowded. The diagram shows the ideal alignment without paper thickness.

    Open step 17 diagram for zooming

  18. Turn over without rotating

    Turn the whole model left to right, keeping the long point up. The broader, still-unfolded face is now in front.

    Open step 18 diagram for zooming

  19. Narrow the remaining edges

    Repeat the two lower-edge folds on this face. Check both sides before continuing: each narrow lower point has folded edges, and the two broad upper wing flaps are still separate. Do not join the two lower tips together.

    Open step 19 diagram for zooming

  20. Precrease the left lower point upward

    Select the LEFT narrow lower point, not the broad upper wing. Fold it upward and outward toward the upper left, with its base near the shoulder line. This is a shaping angle: aim for a raised point visible beside the broad wings, not a point crossing the centre. Press the diagonal crease, then return the point downward.

    Open step 20 diagram for zooming

  21. Inside-reverse the left point

    Slightly separate the layers around the left point at its side opening. Push the point upward INSIDE that pocket along the crease just made. The crease changes direction as the point moves between the surrounding layers. Close the pocket and flatten the raised point at the chosen angle. The broad wing must remain outside it. Do not paste the point across the front face.

    Open step 21 diagram for zooming

  22. Precrease the right lower point upward

    Select the remaining RIGHT narrow lower point. Fold it upward and outward toward the upper right, aiming for a similar height to the first point. Press the crease and reopen it downward. Leave the already raised point in place.

    Open step 22 diagram for zooming

  23. Inside-reverse the right point

    Open the layers at the right side, raise the narrow point inside the pocket along its new crease, then close and flatten. You now need two raised narrow points between two broad wing flaps. Pause here to check their layer positions before making a head.

    Open step 23 diagram for zooming

  24. Precrease a small head

    Choose the raised left narrow point for the neck. Fold only a short piece of its tip downward to mark a beak, then unfold that small fold. Choose the length to suit your paper; the drawing is an example, not a measured requirement. Keep the other raised point straight for the tail.

    Open step 24 diagram for zooming

  25. Inside-reverse the beak

    Ease apart the two sides of the selected neck tip. Push its small end inward and down along the new crease, then close the neck around it. The bent tip stays between the neck layers. Do not fold the entire neck down.

    Open step 25 diagram for zooming

  26. Lower the wings and inspect

    Take the two BROAD upper flaps, one on each face, and lower them gently away from each other. Keep the narrow neck and tail between them. Stop if the body resists; there is no need to force it round or blow into it. Adjust the wing pose gently. This final three-dimensional opening still needs a real paper test. Record the first mismatch, especially a trapped layer or crease that refuses to reverse.

    Open step 26 diagram for zooming

Model and illustration notes

This is the traditional crane. Our wording and vector drawings are original; publisher art was not copied or traced. The sequence uses conventional inside reverse folds for neck and tail throughout. Shaping angles vary; schematic pocket drawings still need physical confirmation.

Helpful folding tips

  • Line up corners before pressing a crease from the middle outwards.
  • Keep the paper facing the same way as the instruction before each fold.

If a fold goes wrong

  • If layers do not line up, return to the last clear step rather than forcing the next fold.
  • Thick paper becomes difficult to fold as layers build up; try a larger, thinner square.
