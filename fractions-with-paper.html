Fractions with Paper Folding

Suggested grades 3–4 · About 20 minutes · One rectangular sheet per student, plus a pencil.

Learning goal

Show why one half and two quarters cover the same amount of the same whole.

Fold and compare

  1. Make halves. Bring the short edges together, crease and unfold. Trace the crease and label both equal parts 1/2.
  2. Make quarters. Fold the paper in half again along the same crease. With the paper still folded, bring the folded edge to the opposite open edge. Unfold completely. You should see four equal strips.
  3. Compare. Shade two adjacent strips on one side of the original crease. The shaded area is both 2/4 of the sheet and 1/2 of the sheet.

Ask students to explain why four pieces of different sizes would not represent four quarters.

Try it

  1. What fraction is one strip?
  2. What fraction is shaded if three strips are shaded?
  3. How many quarters cover the same area as one half?
  4. Is 1/2 of a small sheet always the same area as 1/2 of a larger sheet? Explain.
Answer key
  1. 1/4.
  2. 3/4.
  3. Two quarters. Both fractions cover the same area of the same sheet.
  4. No. Each is half of its own whole, but the sheets have different areas.
