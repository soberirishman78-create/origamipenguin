Origami Penguin - Step-by-Step Tutorial

Fold a penguin with 14 illustrated steps, a vertical centre crease and an outside-reverse head.

Jump to instructionsPrint / save as PDF

Materials

  • One square sheet of paper
  • A flat folding surface

No scissors or glue needed. New to folding? Read the beginner’s guide.

How to read these diagrams

Each numbered picture has a before view and a result or checkpoint. Dark is the coloured paper face; white is the reverse. Blue dashed lines mark active folds and arrows show movement. Grey dotted lines are existing creases. The narrow profile is enlarged after closing the body. The head opening is a schematic detail; the final cap is a flat projection. Read each instruction with its picture.

Step-by-step instructions

  1. Set the starting face

    Use a thin two-colour square, about 15 cm along each side, with a dark side and a white side. Put the DARK side up, with one corner at the top and one toward you. Do not begin with an edge horizontal.

    Open step 1 diagram for zooming

  2. Make the vertical centre crease

    Bring the LEFT corner onto the RIGHT corner. Match the edges, press, then reopen fully. The crease must join the top and bottom corners. This vertical crease will later close the body; a top-to-bottom fold would make the wrong reference line.

    Open step 2 diagram for zooming

  3. Turn to the white face

    Turn the whole square over left to right, keeping the top corner up. The WHITE side is now facing you and the vertical crease is still visible.

    Open step 3 diagram for zooming

  4. Fold the right upper edge partway inward

    Fold the upper-right edge inward, stopping SHORT of the vertical centreline. Keep the top corner fixed. In this illustrated version, the new crease reaches the lower-right sloping edge about two-thirds of the horizontal distance from the centreline to the original right corner. The moved right corner ends near the centre, but does not touch it. Keep a white gap beside the centre crease.

    Open step 4 diagram for zooming

  5. Match the left upper fold

    Fold the upper-left edge inward to match the right fold. Keep both tips equally far from the centreline. The two dark flaps should frame a white central gap. Do not overlap them across the centre or flatten this into a closed kite.

    Open step 5 diagram for zooming

  6. Turn to the other face

    Turn the whole model over left to right with the narrow point still at the top. You now see the mostly dark back of the previous folds. The two wider side corners remain below the top point.

    Open step 6 diagram for zooming

  7. Fold in the right side corner

    Fold the right side corner inward on a VERTICAL crease, through all layers at that corner. In this version the crease is a little beyond halfway from the centreline to that side corner: three-fifths of that horizontal distance. The moved tip stops short of the centre. Keep the crease vertical; do not bring the entire upper edge to the centre.

    Open step 7 diagram for zooming

  8. Match the left side corner

    Repeat the same vertical fold on the left. Make the two folds symmetrical. Their lower endpoints should be level with one another; use those endpoints as the reference for the next fold. Keep the long top point unchanged.

    Open step 8 diagram for zooming

  9. Fold up the bottom point

    Fold the bottom point upward along a horizontal line joining the LOWER endpoints of the two vertical side creases. The point comes to rest above that line, revealing a white triangle. The bottom of the model is now a straight folded edge. Do not aim the tip at the top of the model.

    Open step 9 diagram for zooming

  10. Close the body away from you

    Keep the new white triangle facing you. Fold the LEFT half behind the RIGHT half along the vertical centre crease: this is a mountain fold, away from you. Keep the top point up and the straight bottom edge down. The pale belly must stay on the outside. The drawing enlarges the narrow side profile.

    Open step 10 diagram for zooming

  11. Precrease the head in both directions

    On the closed side profile, bend the top tip outward and downward toward the right to choose a head angle. The crease slopes upward toward the right edge of the neck. The example places the beak roughly level with the top of the neck. Press, reopen, then bend the same cap the opposite way on that same line and reopen again. Both sides of the paired neck layers need a usable crease.

    Open step 11 diagram for zooming

  12. Open the paired neck layers

    Ease the two sides of the neck apart slightly at the prepared head crease. Keep the body and belly folds together below it. Identify the cap above that crease. This opening view is a schematic detail: do not make a new fold where its separated walls are drawn.

    Open step 12 diagram for zooming

  13. Wrap the head over the outside

    Bring the cap outward over the OUTSIDE of the neck as the prepared crease reverses, then close the two neck sides again. The cap wraps around the exterior; do not tuck it between the neck layers. Match the head crease on both sides before flattening gently. The projected result shows a right-facing beak. Hidden wrap layers and paper thickness are not fully represented by the drawing.

    Open step 13 diagram for zooming

  14. Inspect the model and test its base

    Check that the beak points right, the pale belly is outside, and the bottom folded edge remains level. You may gently open the two body sides a little at the base to try balancing it on a table; do not force the paper or invent extra feet.

    Open step 14 diagram for zooming

Helpful folding tips

  • Line up corners before pressing a crease from the middle outwards.
  • Keep the paper facing the same way as the instruction before each fold.

If a fold goes wrong

  • If layers do not line up, return to the last clear step rather than forcing the next fold.
  • Thick paper becomes difficult to fold as layers build up; try a larger, thinner square.
