Handing off a manuscript without handing away its voice

Moving work between writing sessions taught us to carry decisions, not just a vague request to continue.

Note 04 of 52Writing craftOrigami PenguinPublished November 4, 20262 min read

Our book work has moved between sessions and tools. In the trilogy, a handoff described the people, the sequence, the emotional shape of the escape, and the point where the next writer should pick up. In the classic retellings, the request was to carry an established opening forward without losing its style.

“Continue this” sounds simple. It leaves an alarming number of decisions open. A new session may not know which details are fixed, which names have changed, or which apparent inconsistency is an intentional clue.

A useful handoff separates those things. Give the next session a short account of what has happened, the decisions it must preserve, the tone it should maintain, and the actual next deliverable. Include a representative passage. A description such as “warm but tense” is helpful; a page demonstrating that balance is better.

The same habit helps when the next person is simply you, returning after a busy week. Your memory of the project is not a substitute for a record of its decisions. The manuscript will not explain every reason you chose a particular route.

Before closing a substantial writing session, leave a few lines for the next one: where the work stands, what must not change, what is unresolved, and the first task to do. It is a small piece of administration that can save an entire evening of trying to become the same writer again.
