The series page was another piece of the work

A finished volume and a connected series are different experiences for a reader.

Note 13 of 52PublishingOrigami PenguinPublished November 4, 20262 min read

When the Perfect Bathroom Book series listing was submitted, it was still awaiting the platform’s review. The individual publishing work and the series connection did not become complete at exactly the same moment.

That small distinction is easy to overlook from the creator’s side. You know which books belong together. You know that the navy covers are related and that either volume can be enjoyed independently. A new reader only knows what the page in front of them explains.

Series presentation is therefore part of the reading experience before anybody opens the book. It answers practical questions: is there an order, is this a continuation, and does buying one volume commit me to understanding another?

Those answers should follow the actual structure of the books. A collection of independent short pieces does not need to be described like the middle installment of a tightly connected novel. A trilogy should not leave its order to cover-color detective work.

After arranging a series, look at it from outside your own working account. Can a reader find the next book? Is the order clear? Does the description tell them what kind of continuity to expect?

The manuscript may be the biggest part of the project, but it is not the only part a reader encounters. Sometimes the final piece of useful writing is a plain sentence explaining how the books fit together.
