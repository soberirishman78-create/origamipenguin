The Beginner's Guide to Origami

New to paper folding? This guide covers the basics you'll want to know before diving into tutorials: what paper to use, the folding terms and symbols you'll see in diagrams, and a few common mistakes worth avoiding.

Choosing the Right Paper

Standard origami paper (often called "kami") is thin, crisp, and colored on one side. It's the easiest to fold and the best starting point for beginners. As you progress, you may want to try double-sided paper for models where both colors show, or foil-backed paper for shapes that need to hold sharper creases. Size matters too, 6-inch squares are the most common for practice, while larger squares can make complicated folds easier to handle. Save small squares for projects you already know.

Folding Terms & Symbols

Valley Fold

The most basic fold, the paper creases like a "V" toward you. Shown in diagrams as a dashed line.

Mountain Fold

The opposite of a valley fold, the crease points away from you like a peak. Shown as a dash-dot line.

Squash Fold

You open a flap and flatten it symmetrically, "squashing" it into a new shape.

Reverse Fold

A point is tucked inside or reversed along existing creases, commonly used to form heads, beaks, and feet.

Petal Fold

A combination move that lifts a flap upward while narrowing it, often used for wings or petals.

Common Beginner Mistakes

Skipping the Crease

Fold firmly and fully each time, soft or partial creases make later steps harder to align.

Using Paper That's Too Thick

A square cut from printer paper works for simple practice folds. Thin origami paper is easier when a model builds up many layers; avoid stiff cardstock for those models.

Rushing Through Steps

Origami rewards patience. Check each step against the diagram before moving to the next.
