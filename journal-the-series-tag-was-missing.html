Six mysteries were on the wrong shelf

The Kelly Brothers correction showed why a listing title cannot always be trusted to tell the whole story.

Note 28 of 52Building the brandOrigami PenguinPublished November 4, 20262 min read

The first website catalog grouped the Kelly Brothers books using the series information in their listing titles and metadata. That found twenty-two numbered mysteries. It missed six more adventures whose covers identified the same series but whose Amazon titles did not include the tag.

The author noticed the problem: some books down below belonged with the mysteries above. The covers confirmed it. The correction moved those six titles into the series and made the filter show twenty-eight books.

This was not a dramatic technical failure. It was a classification decision based on incomplete evidence. That makes it especially useful to remember. An apparently tidy rule can produce a result that is wrong in a very ordinary way.

The fix was to make series membership an explicit catalog decision. The numbered books kept their order. The additional adventures joined them without inventing book numbers that had not been established. The old total was removed from the display labels.

For your own catalog, give important relationships a field of their own. Do not depend entirely on a phrase happening to appear in a title. Use the title, the cover, the source listing, and the creator’s knowledge together.

Automation can save a lot of sorting time. It still needs a person who knows what the shelf is supposed to contain. The best correction is the one that improves the rule for the next book as well as fixing the current six.
